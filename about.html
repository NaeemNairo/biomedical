About me

I'm a biomedical engineer with a background in mechatronics and three years of product design. At AHL Logics I took four assistive mobility products from first sketch to mass production, which taught me how a device has to be designed, tested and documented before people can rely on it.

I'm now completing an M.S. in Biomedical Engineering at the University of New Haven as a Fulbright Scholar, where I teach in the Instrumentation Laboratory and work on assistive control. My current project uses a standard webcam to turn eye gaze into a proportional control signal for people who can't use their hands; the manuscript is under review.

I'm applying to PhD programs to work on medical devices that are measured, validated and built for daily use.

Research interests

Assistive technology

Low-cost interfaces, like eye-gaze control, that give people with limited hand function control over devices.

Medical device design

Taking devices from concept to validated product, with simulation and testing built into the design process.

Biomedical instrumentation

Sensors and signal acquisition, from EMG to embedded data capture, for measuring how people use devices.
